### Root Cause

**Not a provider outage — resource exhaustion from vendor research daemon.**

1. **Ollama was the bottleneck**: 430 calls, every single one returned 0 tokens with 62-second average latency. The local `llama3.2:3b` model couldn't keep up with the parallel workload from the vendor research daemon running 852 parallel workers.

2. **OpenRouter was degraded**: 16-second average latency (vs normal ~2.5s). The free tier rate limits were being hit by the volume of vendor research requests.

3. **Cerebras and Groq carried the load**: These two handled 1,068 of 1,949 total calls (55%) with sub-2.5s latency, producing 996K tokens. They were the only providers that kept up.

4. **ZAI and Gemini were barely used**: Only 7 calls combined. The fallback chain rarely reached them because Ollama's 62s timeout was consuming the entire timeout budget before falling through.

### Timeline Reconstruction

- 19:26 — Vendor research daemon starts, begins parallel requests across all providers
- 19:26–23:16 — Daemon runs for ~4 hours, inserting 12,304 vendors at ~2,148/hour
- Ollama saturated immediately (single local instance, no GPU acceleration)
- OpenRouter hit rate limits within first hour (200 RPD free tier)
- Cerebras and Groq sustained the workload throughout
- 23:16 — Last vendor insert (daemon likely hit some limit or was killed)

### Recommendations

1. **Remove Ollama from vendor research fallback chain** — it cannot handle parallel workload. Reserve for single interactive queries only.
2. **Add OpenRouter daily safeguard** — already implemented in `core/key_rotation.py` (150/day cap). Verify it activates.
3. **Reduce parallel workers for vendor research** — 852 workers overwhelmed every provider. Cap at 5-10 concurrent research requests.
4. **Add per-provider latency circuit breaker** — if avg latency exceeds 10s over 5 calls, skip that provider for 5 minutes.
5. **Monitor Ollama health** — add a pre-flight check that Ollama is responsive before including it in the rotation.
